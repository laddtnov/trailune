# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A single traveler using Trailune as a personal, private tracker (v1 is local-only, no accounts, no other users). They open it before a trip to plan packing and jot down do's/don'ts, and during/after a trip to log journal entries with photos and watch their world map fill in. Primary context: casual, self-directed use on desktop or mobile browser, not a work tool.

## Product Purpose

Trailune helps someone remember and relive their travel: what they packed, what they did, what they'd tell themselves next time, and where they've been. Success looks like a trips list that feels worth opening, a map that feels like a real trophy case of visited places, and a packing flow that removes "did I forget something" anxiety.

## Brand Personality

Vibrant, warm, travel-app energy — closer to Airbnb (confident rounded shapes, warm photography-led cards, one strong accent color, generous whitespace) crossed with Polarsteps (map-centric, journal/timeline feel, stamped-passport and route visuals as a real visual language, not just a naming convention). Not minimal-SaaS-restrained; the product should feel like it wants to be opened, not like an admin tool.

## Anti-references

Explicitly not: generic AI-slop SaaS — cream/sand near-white backgrounds with a single muted accent, tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, identical icon+heading+text card grids, gradient text, side-stripe borders. Also not a corporate/enterprise dashboard — no dense data tables, no navy-and-gray B2B tool aesthetic.

## Design Principles

- **Warmth through motif, not just palette.** The "passport stamp" idea should show up as real visual language (stamped shapes, postcard/ticket-like cards, route lines) — not just warm-toned CSS variable names on plain rectangles.
- **The map is the emotional core.** It's the product's trophy case. It should read as vibrant and inviting everywhere it's referenced (nav, trips list, map page), not tucked away as a secondary utility page.
- **One confident accent, used boldly.** Given the vibrant direction, the terracotta accent should carry real visual weight (buttons, active states, highlights) rather than being restrained to text links.
- **Empty states are invitations, not dead ends.** An empty trips list or empty packing category should visually pull the user toward the next action, never just say "No items yet." and stop.
- **Motion is intentional, not decorative.** Interactions (adding a trip, checking off a packing item, hovering a map country) should feel responsive and alive, always with a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, visible keyboard focus states, reasonable touch targets. No specific additional constraints beyond standard good practice.
